Leagues & Clubs: Attacking Pace Across Europe

Europe’s “Big Five” domestic leagues share elite talent, yet they play noticeably different styles of football. Because the Bundesliga features 18 clubs playing 34 matches each while the Premier League, La Liga, Serie A, and Ligue 1 have 20 clubs playing 38 matches, comparing raw season totals would undercount German clubs. Normalizing each squad’s output on a per-match basis reveals how leagues and clubs truly separate.

1. Which League Plays the Most Open, High-Scoring Football?

Across all 98 clubs, the Bundesliga averaged 1.52 player-scored goals per team per match (over 3.0 combined goals in a typical fixture), ahead of Serie A (1.38), the Premier League (1.36), and Ligue 1 (1.36). Spanish La Liga was Europe’s tightest defensive competition at 1.22 goals per team per match.

2. Pressing Intensity vs. Scoring Output Across All 98 Clubs

Do teams score more by pressing opponents high up the pitch, or by dominating possession? Plotting presses per match against goals per match highlights a striking tactical divide: possession powerhouses like Manchester City (2.53 goals/match, just 111.3 presses/match) rarely need to press because they keep the ball, whereas Liverpool, Bayern Munich, and RB Leipzig combine relentless pressing with elite scoring. 3. Star Dependence vs. Shared Scoring Among Europe’s Top 15 Attacks

Even among Europe’s highest-scoring clubs, goals are distributed very differently. Bayern Munich (2.71 goals/match) and Real Madrid (2.11 goals/match) leaned heavily on Ballon d’Or contenders Robert Lewandowski (35 league goals) and Karim Benzema (27 league goals), whereas Manchester City won the Premier League with eight different players scoring 5+ goals and no single player exceeding 15.
